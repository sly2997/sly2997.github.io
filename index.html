Impact of Imperata cylindrica on the Performance of Water Based Mud in Saline Medium

S. Igbafe, A. I. Igbafe, A. Ameloko

Spear grass (Imperata cylindrica) was pulverised and mixed into bentonite water-based mud, then tested for rheology, pH, mud weight and fluid loss at room temperature, 70 °C and 100 °C with salt contamination from 5 to 25 g. At the same 1 g loading it reduced fluid loss more effectively than xanthan gum and improved other mud properties under saline conditions.

Production and Characterization of Chrysophyllum albidum Seed Oil Derived Bio-lubricant for the Formulation of Oil-Based Drilling Mud

S. Igbafe, A. A. Azuokwu, A. I. Igbafe

A polyol-ester bio-lubricant was synthesised from African star apple (Chrysophyllum albidum) seed oil as a replacement for diesel in the continuous phase of oil-based mud. Density, viscosity, pour point and flash point met ASTM standards and compared favourably with diesel, with lower toxicity and better biodegradability.

Comparative Performances of Three Copper-Based Electrocatalysts towards Hydrogen Evolution Reaction in Alkaline Water Electrolysis

Anselm Iuebego Igbafe, Lucky Chukwudi Eneh, Sylvia Igbafe, Olusola Adedayo Adesina

Nickel-iron and nickel-copper-iron composite electrocatalysts were electrodeposited onto copper foam and used as cathodes in alkaline water electrolysis, then compared on hydrogen yield, purity, overpotential, Tafel slope and stability. Hydrogen purity reached 88.36, 88.59 and 92.13 % for the copper foam, nickel-iron and nickel-copper-iron cathodes, the ternary composite performing best with 78.3 % energy efficiency after 20 hours.

doi.org/10.53982/ajerd.2026.0902.08-j

Synthesis and Application of Starch-Modified Chitosan-Silver Nanoparticle Composite as Green Corrosion Inhibitor for Mild Steel in Acidic Media

Anthonia Fidamilare Fidudusola, Sylvia Igbafe, Anselm Iuebego Igbafe

A green corrosion inhibitor was built from waste cassava peel starch, chitosan from snail shells and green-synthesised silver nanoparticles, then tested on mild steel in dilute hydrochloric acid. The composite reached up to 97 % inhibition efficiency by forming a protective film, the nanoparticles improving stability and the biopolymers keeping it non-toxic.

Semi Empirical Modelling of Alkaline Water Electrolysis Green Hydrogen Using Biosynthesized Lye and Caustic Soda Electrolytes

Anthony Owoicho Ijiga, Sylvia Igbafe, Akeem Aderibigbe Adebomehin, Anselm Iuebego Igbafe

Semi-empirical models were fitted for cell voltage, Faraday efficiency and gas purity in alkaline water electrolysis, using lye from charred plantain peel and caustic soda from seawater at 25 to 35 g/L and 45 to 85 °C. Most models fitted well, with R² between 0.96 and 0.999.

Green Hydrogen Synthesis from Human Urine as Sustainable Bioenergy Resources

Blossom Odili, Sylvia Igbafe, Adeyinka Sikiru Yusuff, Akeem Aderibigbe Adebomehin, Anselm Iuebego Igbafe

Hydrogen was produced by electrolysis of the urea in human urine, modelled empirically across three electrolyte concentrations and five temperatures from 45 to 85 °C. Cathode gas reached 99.88 % hydrogen, with output highest at 35 wt. and 65 °C, and most models fitting with R² between 0.96 and 0.99.

Applications of Green Inhibitors of Sugarcane Bagasse and Garcinia Kola Fruit Pod for Mild Steel Natural Gas Pipeline Corrosion Inhibition in Acidic Medium

Teddy Ekimimosugha Jemine, Sylvia Igbafe, Adekunle Timisiriyu Adeniyi, Anselm Iuebego Igbafe

Ethanol extracts of sugarcane bagasse and garcinia kola fruit pod were tested as green corrosion inhibitors for mild steel gas pipeline in acid, using a central composite design across concentration, exposure time and temperature. Best efficiencies were 92 % and 90 % respectively, with garcinia kola the stronger inhibitor overall.

Optimization of Green Hydrogen Production in Alkaline Water Electrolysis with Bio Synthesized Lye and Caustic Soda Electrolytes using Taguchi OA

Anthony Owoicho Ijiga, Sylvia Igbafe, Akeem Aderibigbe Adebomehin, Anselm Iuebego Igbafe

A Taguchi orthogonal array was used to find the conditions that maximise hydrogen volume in alkaline water electrolysis with plantain-peel lye and seawater-derived caustic soda. The optimum was 30 g/L, 15 minutes, 85 °C and 7 V for KOH, and 35 g/L, 15 minutes, 45 °C and 7 V for NaOH.

How I work

Published data, not synthetic

The formulation data comes from peer-reviewed studies, extracted by hand. Where a dataset is partly synthetic, it says so.

Test conditions on every row

Temperature, aging condition and concentration travel with each measurement. A model trained across mixed protocols learns laboratory differences and calls them chemistry.

A cited report closes every project

IMRaD structure, references managed in Zotero, and a limitations section written to be read rather than skipped.

Data and notebooks published

Each project ships its dataset, its notebook and a README, so the analysis can be checked and repeated.
